### B. Company Background

DataForge Academy is an ed-tech company founded in 2019 that focuses on developing digital 
skills in technology and data, ranging from coding to cybersecurity. This includes offering 
regular courses, mini-courses, boot camps, and certifications.

In marketing its products, the company employs the following strategies.

1. Top of the Funnel (TOFU)
 
The goal of this stage is to reach as many people as possible and make them aware of and 
familiar with the company’s brand.

Efforts undertaken include: <br>
- Social Media Content: through long-form or shorter videos, image posts, and content 
tailored to social media trends so that people can stay informed and feel represented by 
the content that is uploaded. 
- SEO and Articles: creating articles that typically provide comprehensive explanations of 
an issue or phenomenon in written format. Most of this content addresses the most 
frequently asked questions about technology and data. 
- Reach Advertising: Creating ads and distributing them through paid channels such as 
Google Ads, Meta Ads, and others that include engaging content as a way to attract 
audiences to click on the ads and learn more about the services offered.

2. Middle of the Funnel (MOFU)

The goal of this stage is to encourage audiences who are already familiar with the Company’s services to trust them and begin considering the available services. It is at this stage that the audience transforms into leads as a result of taking an initial conversion 
action (Lead Magnet).

Examples of such efforts include: <br>
- Free webinars: hosting online sessions at no cost whatsoever—participants simply need 
to register to receive an introduction to the material or discuss current issues surrounding technology and data with trusted experts. This serves as a bridge to help people become more familiar with and interested in the core services provided. 
- Free e-book: Provides practical materials that can be downloaded at any time through 
the website after officially registering and filling out your contact information. 
- Email Nurturing: Sends a series of automated messages containing the latest news on technology and data, program offers, and other content to stay connected with audiences who have submitted their contact information.

3. Bottom of the Funnel (BOFU)

The goal of this stage is to convince leads to sign up for services and make a purchase.  
Efforts include: <br>
- Social Proof and Alumni Stories: Featuring video testimonials from alumni who have successfully switched careers, complete with a comparison of their backgrounds before
and after graduation. 
- Create a sense of urgency: use a batch registration system, seat quotas, and Early Bird
promotions with a countdown timer. 
- Retargeting ads: Display targeted ads to people who have visited the registration page or 
nearly completed registration (abandoned checkout), offering additional discount codes or compelling testimonials. 

Although all three strategies in the Top of the Funnel (TOFU) aim to build brand awareness, they actually differ in terms of their target audiences.

Social media content creates demand; in other words, audiences who log into their social media accounts are looking for entertainment, not to sign up for a course. Therefore, the content on this channel is designed to spark people’s emotions or curiosity through topics such as “salaries in the IT industry” or “success stories of career switches in the data field,” so that viewers become aware of these opportunities and develop a desire to acquire those skills. 

Meanwhile, SEO and articles are tasked with building authority through education. People who
have questions about technology and data will use search engines to find answers, meaning
they’re still in the early stages and highly curious about the field. At this stage, they haven’t yet decided to enroll in a course. The content on this channel is designed to provide reliable and engaging explanations so that the audience will easily remember the brand as a trusted source when they become serious and committed to learning more.

Reach ads are a different story. Their purpose is to capture existing demand. Ads placed on 
specific channels can appear when the audience enters trigger keywords. 

### C. Digital Marketing


#### Google Ads

Google’s search engine features a specialized advertising platform called Google Ads. When you run ads through Google Ads, you pay per click. This means your ad can appear when triggered by specific keywords that users enter into the search bar, directing them directly to the relevant landing page. These keywords typically indicate an interest in signing up for a program, such as the use of words like “learn,” “course,” or “price.” 

This is why reach-based ads like Google Ads serve as a direct bridge from the top of the funnel to the middle of the funnel to generate leads.

##### Ad payment mechanisms

1. Ads only need to be uploaded once

Ads that have been created and approved by Google will be saved, and the campaign will 
continue to run. 

2. Ads can run every day

The default settings in Google Ads set ads to run for a full 24 hours every day while the campaign is active. Advertisers can also set specific times for their ads to run.As long as the daily budget hasn’t been exhausted and there’s sufficient account balance, the system will continue to show ads to relevant audiences.

3. Ads can be clicked every day

Ads can be clicked every day because searches are happening constantly. There are millions 
of users typing in keywords that can trigger ads.

##### Impact of Ad Rank and Cost on ad impressions

Ad Rank determines whether an ad is eligible to win the auction and where it appears in 
the search results. 

The ad budget determines how often an ad is allowed to participate in the auction 
throughout the day.

1. The Role of Ad Rank (Quality and Relevance)
  
It determines auction success through a combination of the bid, ad quality (text relevance,
landing page experience, estimated click-through rate), and the user’s search context. If an ad’s relevance is very high, its Ad Rank can be high, and the ad will appear at the top when someone performs a search.

2. The Role of Budget (Auction Participation Capacity)

The budget will limit the frequency of ad impressions. If 100 people search for a keyword 
that triggers an ad in a single day, with an average cost per click (CPC) of $2, and the daily budget is $50, then the budget will only be enough for 25 clicks.

Google’s system will adjust the budget amount based on ad impressions. Google can manage the pace so that ads only participate in some search auctions to prevent the budget 
from being exhausted in a matter of minutes.

Therefore, by increasing the budget by $500 per day while maintaining the same ad quality, 
Google will allow the ads to participate in more search auctions. As a result, the number of impressions and clicks will automatically surge.

##### Factors affecting CPC

Here’s a more detailed explanation of the factors that influence the cost per click for ads placed on Google Ads.

1. Setting a Maximum CPC Bid

You can manually set the maximum amount you’re willing to pay for a single click or let an 
automated algorithm—called Smart Bidding—handle it based on conversions or your daily 
budget.

2. Ad Rank Calculation During the Auction

When a user enters a keyword into the search bar, Google calculates the Ad Rank for each
advertiser competing for that keyword. This score is calculated not only based on the maximum CPC bid but also on a combination of the following factors. <br>
- Quality Score, which is calculated based on ad relevance, estimated click-through rate
(CTR), and the quality of the landing page experience.  
- Search context and ad presence, including the device used, location, time, and the
impact of ad extensions or phone numbers. 
The higher the Ad Rank, the greater the likelihood that the ad will appear in the top position of the search results.

3. Actual CPC Calculation

When an ad successfully appears in the top position, Google immediately calculates the
Actual CPC if a user clicks on the ad. This cost is calculated based on the Ad Rank of the
competitor immediately below the ad’s position, divided by the Quality Score, and then
added to $0.01 or the smallest applicable currency unit.

This mechanism benefits ads with a high-Quality Score, ads with high relevance and a high-quality landing page can pay a much lower Actual CPC compared to competitors whose ads
are less relevant.

4. Click triggering and validity filtering

When a user clicks on an ad and is redirected to the landing page, Google records the
potential charge based on the Actual CPC. However, before deducting the amount from the
balance, Google’s system checks the validity of the click. If the click is found to be invalid or attributed to bots or spam, the charge is canceled.

5. Budget Deduction and Billing

Once the click is deemed valid, the charge equal to the Actual CPC will immediately be
deducted from the remaining daily campaign budget for that day.

#### Role

The company has a Digital Marketing Division, which includes various sub-divisions responsible for marketing efforts in their respective fields, such as the Social Media Lead, SEO Lead, Advertising Lead, Marketing Automation Lead, Content Strategist, Media Planner, and Marketing Analyst.

The Social Media Lead, Advertising Lead, and Marketing Automation Lead are responsible for 
creating content on their respective channels based on previously developed strategic plans to achieve the company’s goals.

The Content Strategist is responsible for planning and providing guidance regarding the content and its distribution across each channel to reach a wider audience.

A Media Planner is responsible for managing ad placements to reach the right audience as cost effectively as possible.

A Marketing Analyst is responsible for analyzing all campaign data to assess its impact on the business as a whole.

#### Digital Marketing Workflow

The workflow within the Digital Marketing division, which operates in cycles and connects all sub-divisions, is described in the following sequence.

1. Initial Research and Target Setting

This stage involves the Marketing Analyst reviewing past sales data and market trends to determine future campaign targets. <br> 
This is where KPIs are determined, such as setting success metrics like cost per customer
acquisition (CPA) or return on ad spend (ROAS) targets.

2. Planning Media and Budget

This stage involves the Media Planner in determining the most effective platforms for advertising based on the Marketing Analyst’s analysis. <br>
They are also responsible for allocating the advertising budget across each platform and determining the ad schedule and reach targets.

3. Developing Message Concepts and Assets

This stage involves the Content Strategist, who translates the business objectives and  platform specifications from the Media Planner’s brief into guidelines for content reation. <br> 
They must ensure that the message to be conveyed aligns with the content on each platform.

4. Producing advertising materials and landing pages
 
Only then does content creation begin. Copywriters write ad copy, while video photographers and editors create images or videos tailored to each platform’s format. The Web/CRO team prepares a dedicated landing page for the offer, complete with a form or a purchase button (Call to Action).

5. Launch and Daily Optimization

Once the campaign is running, the media planner monitors daily performance and shifts the
advertising budget toward the ads or platforms that deliver the best results.

6. Comprehensive Evaluation and Improvements

After the campaign ends, the marketing analyst calculates the total profit, budget effectiveness, and audience behavior on each platform. The results of this analysis serve as the basis for the marketing planner and content strategist to refine the messaging strategy and channel selection for the next campaign.
